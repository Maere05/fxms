Diagnostic Analysis of Slip-Velocity Bounce-Back Integration and Force Evaluation for High-Re Aerodynamics in FluidX3D1. Introduction and Diagnostic ContextThe integration of advanced wall-modeling techniques, such as the Slip-Velocity Bounce-Back (SVBB) scheme, into lattice Boltzmann method (LBM) solvers is essential for simulating high-Reynolds-number external aerodynamics within strict computational constraints. In the context of FluidX3D, operating under a strict 10 GB VRAM limit utilizing the D3Q19 velocity set, a Single-Relaxation-Time (SRT) collision operator, and FP16C memory compression, the simulation of a 25-degree Ahmed body at a reported Reynolds number of $\text{Re} \approx 2.82 \times 10^6$ presents a formidable challenge. The current diagnostic profile of the SVBB implementation yields a profound paradox: the wall model is computationally active and modifying populations, yet it fails to manifest any material change in the macroscopic aerodynamic drag.The diagnostic telemetry confirms this active state over a 50-step sample. The domain contains 33,636,600 wall-adjacent cells producing 167,371,500 solid neighbor links. Of these, 16,192,500 links (9.7%) correspond to the voxelized Ahmed body (TYPE_S|TYPE_X), while the vast majority, 151,179,000 links (90.3%), belong to the plain solid floor (TYPE_S). The SVBB modification successfully engages ~167,361,502 of these links. Crucially, earlier hypotheses suggesting that the SVBB logic was collapsing to zero slip due to numerical clamping are demonstrably false; reversal-clamped links account for merely ~9,998 occurrences (0.006%), and positivity clamps are entirely absent. The algorithm successfully enforces an average final slip velocity of $\approx 0.01376$ lattice units, achieving a remarkably high average slip ratio of $\approx 0.921$ relative to the adjacent fluid velocity.Despite this aggressive modification of the near-wall velocity field, the integrated Momentum Exchange Method (MEM) body force remains effectively inert. The baseline (no-wall-model) mean drag registers at $83.4289 \text{ N}$ (mean $\text{Cd} = 0.759886$), while the SVBB-enabled run yields $83.4335 \text{ N}$ (mean $\text{Cd} = 0.759928$). This represents a microscopic drag increase of $+0.0046 \text{ N}$, equating to a $\text{Cd}$ deviation of merely $+0.0000418$.The persistence of this inert force output requires an exhaustive technical investigation encompassing LBM algorithmic timing, MEM Galilean invariance, vector directionality, localized subgrid-scale (SGS) viscosity behavior, and the aerodynamic realities of Cartesian voxelization.2. LBM Architecture and MEM Timing DynamicsThe foundational architecture of FluidX3D is built upon highly optimized in-place streaming schemes, specifically the Esoteric Pull and Esoteric Push algorithms. While these algorithms provide a 50% reduction in memory overhead by requiring only a single copy of the density distribution functions (DDFs), they dictate a rigid sequence of memory read/write operations that heavily influences boundary condition application and subsequent force evaluation.2.1 Population State Within the stream_collide() KernelIn the standard Esoteric Pull implementation, the streaming and collision steps are fused into a single thread-safe kernel, stream_collide(). For a fluid node located at $x_f$, the algorithm pulls incoming populations $f_i$ from adjacent nodes, performs the SRT collision relaxation to compute the post-collision state $f_i^*$, and writes the outgoing populations directly back to global memory.When a fluid node is adjacent to a solid boundary (TYPE_S), the standard pull operation is intercepted by the mid-grid bounce-back rule. The fluid node cannot pull a valid distribution from the solid node; instead, it reads its own outgoing post-collision population from the previous step, reverses its direction, and sets it as the incoming population.The implementation of the SVBB model intervenes at this exact moment. A fictitious wall velocity, $u_{wall}$, derived from the Werner-Wengle wall law, is injected into the reflected population to enforce a specific wall shear stress. The correction takes the theoretical form:
$$f_{\bar{i}}(x_f, t + \Delta t) = f_i^*(x_f, t) - 2 w_i \rho \frac{c_i \cdot u_{wall}}{c_s^2}$$Because this modification is applied within stream_collide(), the array fi residing in the global device memory is successfully updated with the SVBB-corrected populations. The diagnostic confirmation of ~167.3 million nonzero slip links definitively proves that the modified DDFs are surviving the streaming step and persisting in the global state.2.2 The Disconnect in update_force_field()The critical failure point resides in how these modified populations are translated into macroscopic aerodynamic forces. FluidX3D computes boundary forces via an abstracted Momentum Exchange Method (MEM). This computation is not performed concurrently within stream_collide(); rather, it is executed by a separate, subsequent kernel invocation triggered by lbm.update_force_field().The standard MEM calculates the force exerted on a solid boundary node $x_b$ by a fluid node $x_f$ by summing the momentum exchanged across the intersecting lattice links:$$F_{MEM} = \frac{\Delta x}{\Delta t} \sum_{x_b} \sum_{i} c_i \left[ f_i(x_b, t) + f_{\bar{i}}(x_f, t) \right]$$
When update_force_field() executes, it reads the current state of fi from global memory. Because this occurs after stream_collide(), it correctly reads the post-SVBB reflected populations $f_{\bar{i}}$. However, reading the correct populations is insufficient. The standard MEM formula is fundamentally derived under the strict assumption of a stationary, no-slip wall ($u_{wall} = 0$).2.3 Galilean Invariance Failure in the Momentum Exchange MethodThe literature on LBM boundary conditions extensively documents that the conventional MEM violates Galilean invariance when applied to moving walls or boundaries with a slip velocity. When a wall possesses a velocity, there is a net mass and momentum transfer across the boundary during the streaming step that the standard formulation ignores.To extract the physically correct force from a moving or slipping wall, the MEM must be structurally adapted to subtract the initial momentum of the mass transfer. The corrected momentum exchange force takes the form:
$$F_{correct} = \sum_{x_b} \sum_{i} c_i \left[ f_i(x_b, t) + f_{\bar{i}}(x_f, t) \right] - \sum_{x_b} \sum_{i} c_i \left( 2 \rho_B w_i \frac{c_i \cdot u_{wall}}{c_s^2} \right)$$If FluidX3D’s update_force_field() kernel has not been explicitly rewritten to include this correction term for the SVBB velocity $u_{wall}$, the integration of forces will collapse. The kernel will read the SVBB-modified DDFs, but because the formula expects a stationary wall, the injected momentum $c_i \cdot u_{wall}$ either registers as a false unphysical force or geometrically cancels out across the symmetric boundaries of the Ahmed body.Therefore, force accumulation should either be modified to adapt the standard MEM formula, or, ideally, the local force momentum correction should be accumulated directly inside the stream_collide() SVBB branch, completely bypassing the legacy update_force_field() logic for slip boundaries.3. Directionality, Sign Conventions, and Symmetric CancellationThe efficacy of the SVBB momentum injection is entirely dictated by the rigorous adherence to LBM sign conventions. A localized error in index directionality or dot product signs will invert the physical meaning of the wall shear stress, transforming friction reduction into artificial propulsion or resulting in complete zero-sum cancellation during force aggregation.3.1 Verification of the SVBB Sign ConventionThe theoretical momentum correction term for halfway bounce-back on a moving boundary is universally defined as:$$\Delta f_{\bar{i}} = - 2 w_i \rho \frac{c_i \cdot u_{wall}}{c_s^2}$$Given that the LBM speed of sound squared is $c_s^2 = 1/3$, this simplifies to:$$\Delta f_{\bar{i}} = - 6 w_i \rho (c_i \cdot u_{wall})$$The FluidX3D SVBB implementation utilizes the following code logic:delta = -6 * w(reflected_i) * rhoB * dot(c_wall, u_wall)This expression requires forensic evaluation of the variables reflected_i and c_wall. In LBM lattice indexing (such as D3Q19), the lattice weights are isotropic for opposite directions, meaning $w_i = w_{\bar{i}}$. Thus, w(reflected_i) is mathematically safe and identical to the incoming link weight.However, the definition of the vector c_wall is the critical vulnerability. In LBM convention, $c_i$ represents the discrete velocity vector of the population traveling from the fluid node into the solid boundary. If c_wall is populated using this fluid-to-solid vector ($c_i$), then the formulation dot(c_wall, u_wall) is physically correct, and the leading negative sign properly subtracts the wall's momentum from the fluid, mimicking shear stress.Conversely, if c_wall is populated using the reflected vector ($c_{\bar{i}}$), which points from the solid back into the fluid, the sign of the entire term is inadvertently reversed since $c_{\bar{i}} = -c_i$.Vector Definition (c_wall)Dot Product PhysicsForce ImplicationMacroscopic ResultFluid-to-Solid ($c_i$)Retards fluid momentumAdds drag to solid bodyCorrect physical behaviorSolid-to-Fluid ($c_{\bar{i}}$)Accelerates fluidAdds thrust to solid bodyArtificial propulsionIf the SVBB logic applies the correction to the returning population slot but utilizes the returning vector $c_{\bar{i}}$ to calculate the dot product without dropping the leading negative sign, the model mathematically acts as an accelerator rather than a friction retarder.3.2 Symmetric Cancellation in MEM AggregationEven if the sign convention is strictly correct, the diagnostic data revealing virtually unchanged drag forces (+0.0046 N) strongly implies a symmetrical cancellation effect during MEM accumulation.The Ahmed body is a closed, 3D aerodynamic shape. The function lbm.object_force(TYPE_S|TYPE_X) globally sums the Cartesian force components over every flagged boundary cell. If the SVBB implementation injects the fictitious wall velocity $u_{wall}$ isotropically, or if the tangential vector calculation fails to respect the local surface normal perfectly, the momentum modifications will geometrically oppose each other.For example, if a uniform magnitude of $u_{wall}$ is applied horizontally along the roof, the side panels, and the underbody, the force reduction on the roof will be correctly registered in the X-direction. However, if the same model miscalculates the tangent on the front stagnation face and injects an equivalent lateral momentum, the forces will sum to near zero over the closed control volume of the vehicle. A diagnostic extraction of the maximum and minimum absolute population deltas per cell is required to ascertain whether massive local forces are simply canceling out in the global summation.4. Tangential Velocity and the Physics of Voxelized Wall GeometryThe SVBB wall model relies intrinsically on the calculation of the wall-tangential fluid velocity $u_t$. The precision of this calculation dictates the accuracy of the applied wall shear stress. In a 10 GB VRAM simulation, the Ahmed body is resolved using a coarse Cartesian voxel grid, which fundamentally conflicts with the geometric assumptions of the wall function.4.1 Discrete Link Normals vs. Reconstructed Surface NormalsThe diagnostic data indicates that the SVBB model calculates tangential velocity relative to the discrete lattice link normals. In LBM, links align with fixed lattice directions (e.g., purely horizontal $(1,0,0)$ or vertical $(0,0,1)$).The 25-degree rear slant of the Ahmed body poses a severe problem for this approach. On a Cartesian grid, a 25-degree surface does not contain any links with a 25-degree normal; instead, the surface is represented as a "staircase" composed exclusively of 90-degree vertical and horizontal voxel faces.If the tangential velocity $u_t$ is computed using the discrete link normal of a horizontal staircase step, the SVBB algorithm assumes the physical wall is perfectly flat and horizontal. It calculates the tangent as purely parallel to the X-axis. Consequently, it injects a horizontal slip velocity that drives the fluid directly into the vertical face of the next staircase step.This creates severe micro-stagnation zones in the internal corners of the staircase geometry. The injected slip velocity, rather than smoothly reducing skin friction along a 25-degree slant, generates intense local pressure spikes inside the steps. The intended friction reduction is entirely consumed by the artificial pressure drag induced by forcing the flow into the voxel corners. To function correctly on arbitrary geometries, SVBB must not rely on discrete link normals; it must compute $u_t$ relative to a reconstructed, continuous macroscopic surface normal derived from the object's geometry.4.2 Floor-Dominated Link ContaminationA critical finding in the diagnostics is the overwhelming dominance of plain solid floor links. Out of 167.3 million total solid links, 151.1 million (90.3%) belong to the floor, while only 16.1 million (9.7%) correspond to the Ahmed body.While the floor links are correctly excluded from the object_force(TYPE_S|TYPE_X) aggregation , applying the SVBB model to these links fundamentally alters the domain's global aerodynamics. A wall function applied to the floor will artificially thin the boundary layer approaching the Ahmed body, modifying the momentum thickness and increasing the dynamic pressure of the flow impacting the vehicle's stagnation plane.If the SVBB model is successfully reducing skin friction on the Ahmed body, the resulting drop in drag may be perfectly offset by the increased stagnation pressure caused by the accelerated floor boundary layer. Applying identical wall functions to both an aerodynamic vehicle and a ground plane is physically inconsistent, as the boundary layer states (turbulent vs. developing) are vastly different. To isolate the efficacy of the SVBB model on the Ahmed body, the floor must be temporarily reverted to a standard TYPE_S no-slip condition or a free-slip symmetry plane, removing it from the SVBB logic entirely.5. Force Contribution Magnitude and Aerodynamic Drag BreakdownTo understand why a massive average slip ratio of $\approx 0.921$ yields a drag coefficient change of merely $+0.0000418$, the physical magnitude of the injected momentum must be compared against the macroscopic pressure drag of the voxelized vehicle.5.1 Estimating the SVBB Force ImpulseThe diagnostic data reports an average final slip of $u_w \approx 0.01376$ lattice units. We can estimate the theoretical maximum force reduction this slip can produce.The momentum correction per link is governed by $\Delta f = 6 w_i \rho_B (c_i \cdot u_w)$. Assuming an average link weight for the D3Q19 lattice of $w_i = 1/18$, a boundary density $\rho_B \approx 1.0$, and an optimal alignment where $(c_i \cdot u_w) \approx u_w$:$$\Delta f_{link} \approx 6 \times \left(\frac{1}{18}\right) \times 1.0 \times 0.01376 \approx 0.00458 \text{ lattice force units per link}$$The Ahmed body contains $\approx 16,192,500$ links. However, skin friction drag in the X-direction is only generated by surfaces parallel to the flow (the roof, underbody, and sides), representing roughly 30% of the total links ($\sim 4.8$ million links). If the slip was perfectly aligned to reduce friction on these surfaces, the total theoretical force reduction would be:$$\Delta F_{total} \approx 4,800,000 \times 0.00458 \approx 21,984 \text{ lattice force units}$$While this seems substantial, the multi-directional nature of the DDFs and the geometric cancellation of the voxel staircases severely diminish this theoretical maximum. When projected onto the X-axis and integrated via MEM, the true force delta shrinks exponentially, leading to the minuscule $0.0046 \text{ N}$ variation observed in SI units.5.2 Skin Friction vs. Pressure Drag DominanceThe core of the paradox lies in the composition of the aerodynamic drag. The baseline simulation reports a massive mean drag of 83.4289 N, corresponding to $\text{Cd} \approx 0.759$.In fluid dynamics, total drag $C_d$ is the sum of pressure (form) drag $C_{dp}$ and skin friction drag $C_{df}$. For a highly streamlined body, $C_{df}$ dominates. For bluff bodies like the Ahmed geometry, $C_{dp}$ dictates the total drag.The SVBB algorithm, driven by the Werner-Wengle law, is exclusively a skin friction modifier. It alters the velocity gradient at the wall to reduce viscous shear stress. It does not—and mathematically cannot—alter the macroscopic pressure field that causes form drag, unless it delays separation.In a perfectly resolved physical simulation of an Ahmed body, skin friction drag accounts for approximately 5% to 10% of the total drag ($C_{df} \approx 0.03$). Even if the SVBB model achieved absolute perfection and reduced skin friction by 50%, the total $C_d$ would only drop by $\sim 0.015$. Given the massive absolute scale of the pressure drag in this simulation (driven by a $C_d$ of 0.76), a variance in skin friction is mathematically drowned out by the turbulent noise and pressure fluctuations of the massive wake. The SVBB model is modifying populations, but the aerodynamic penalty it addresses (skin friction) is too small a fraction of the total drag to register a material change.6. Dimensional Consistency in Wall-Law Turbulence ModelingThe most profound theoretical vulnerability in the provided diagnostic data lies in the interaction between the localized subgrid-scale (SGS) turbulence model and the algebraic wall function. The scaling of the slip velocity relies directly on the fluid's kinematic viscosity, and dimensional inconsistency here will mathematically neutralize the SVBB model.6.1 The Werner-Wengle MechanicsThe SVBB utilizes the Werner-Wengle (WW) wall model. The WW formulation is an algebraic equation designed to estimate the wall shear stress $\tau_w$ based on the tangential velocity $u_t$ at the first off-wall node located at a distance $\Delta y$. Once $\tau_w$ is estimated (using power-law or linear piecewise functions), the LBM must reverse-calculate the fictitious slip velocity $u_w$ required to enforce this stress boundary condition.The fundamental relationship between wall shear stress and the velocity gradient near a wall is:$$\tau_w = \rho \nu_{wall} \frac{\partial u}{\partial y} \approx \rho \nu_{wall} \frac{u_t - u_w}{\Delta y}$$Rearranging to solve for the required slip velocity yields:$$u_w = u_t - \frac{\tau_w \Delta y}{\rho \nu_{wall}}$$6.2 The Smagorinsky $\nu_{eff}$ MismatchThe diagnostics reveal a critical parameter mismatch: the simulation relies on a local average effective viscosity of $\nu_{eff} \approx 0.00108$ near the wall links, while the molecular viscosity is vastly lower at $\nu_{mol} \approx 7.55 \times 10^{-6}$.FluidX3D employs a Smagorinsky-Lilly SGS model , where the effective viscosity is the sum of molecular and turbulent eddy viscosity: $\nu_{eff} = \nu_{mol} + \nu_t$. The Smagorinsky model computes $\nu_t$ based on the magnitude of the local strain rate tensor.Near a solid boundary, physical turbulent eddy viscosity $\nu_t$ must decay to zero (scaling with $y^3$ in the viscous sublayer). However, the standard Smagorinsky model lacks van Driest wall damping. The high mean shear generated by the no-slip condition, combined with the extreme artificial shear produced by the sharp corners of the voxel staircases, causes the Smagorinsky model to drastically overpredict $\nu_t$ at the wall boundary.The diagnostic data shows that if $\nu_{eff}$ is utilized in the slip equation denominator, the calculation becomes:$$u_w = u_t - \frac{\tau_w \Delta y}{\rho (0.00108)}$$Because $0.00108$ is roughly 143 times larger than the physical molecular viscosity ($7.55 \times 10^{-6}$), the subtraction term $\frac{\tau_w \Delta y}{\rho \nu_{eff}}$ is mathematically strangled. The term becomes infinitesimally small, forcing the equation into a state where $u_w \approx u_t$.This explains the extremely high average slip ratio of $\approx 0.921$. The SVBB is injecting a slip velocity that is almost identical to the external fluid velocity, effectively turning the boundary into an artificial free-slip condition. However, because the molecular momentum transfer scales inversely with this massive effective viscosity, the LBM registers virtually no momentum exchange $\Delta f$.To achieve dimensional and physical consistency, the SVBB implementation must exclusively utilize the molecular viscosity ($\nu_{mol}$) when dividing the wall stress to find the slip velocity. The Werner-Wengle formulation already explicitly accounts for the turbulent momentum flux within its empirical derivation of $\tau_w$. Re-introducing the Smagorinsky $\nu_t$ into the denominator double-counts the turbulent eddy viscosity, collapsing the mathematical mechanism that generates the wall force.7.
